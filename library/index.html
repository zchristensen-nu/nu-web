Northeastern components

Every component the northeastern.edu sites are built from, grouped by the job it does: the specimen on both grounds and at both widths, what it takes, when to use it, its markup and the pages that use it.

186 components: 177 stable · 9 one page. Generated from the system's source on every build.

Basics

Type, media and marks: the parts every component is made of.

  • IconOne drawn glyph from the system set.
  • LogoThe official Northeastern University lockup, copied from the live site and never redrawn.
  • HeadingHeadings are FF Real Head Semilight (300) at the three display sizes and Regular (400) from h1 down.
  • TextRunning copy in five roles.
  • NumeralA figure set as type, Light with tabular numerals, at display scale.
  • ImageA photograph in a square-cornered frame.
  • MetaListThe facts that sit under a title.
  • VideoA film in a square-cornered frame.
  • KeyValueDefinition rows for the facts of a program, place or event.
  • ScrimThe only way text sits on a photograph.
  • RuleA full-width hairline.
  • TagA short static label.
  • ChipA toggle that selects one value.
  • QuoteA person’s own words set as display type, 22a’s voices.
  • FigureA photograph or film with its caption and credit.
  • Label, Hint, ErrorTextThe three text atoms a form field is built from.
  • ProgressLine22a's hairline rail with a filling bar.
  • Skeleton and SpinnerWaiting states.

Actions and links

Buttons, links and the controls that sit beside media.

  • IconButtonOne universally known action as a 44px circle.
  • MediaControlThe pause/play circle that every self-moving thing carries (hero film, rotating spotlight, carousel), optionally with the ProgressLine that times the current slide and a short position label.
  • ArrowLinkA standalone link that leads somewhere.
  • ButtonOne action, as a pill.
  • LinkAn inline link inside running text.
  • PaginationNumbered pages between previous and next steps, for result lists that need addresses (program search, site search).
  • SocialLinksThe university's or a unit's social accounts as a row of IconButtons, each named for the network.
  • ShareLinksShare this page.
  • FilmPlayerA film that plays in place, in the square frame of whatever holds it.

Navigation

Headers, menus, footers and the ways through a page or a site.

  • GlobalHeaderThe university bar every official site carries, unaltered.
  • MegaMenuOne section's panel, hung full width from the GlobalHeader when its NavTrigger opens.
  • SearchOverlaySite-wide search, opened from the GlobalHeader's search button.
  • MobileMenuThe GlobalHeader below 960px.
  • LocalHeaderOn unit sites the unit's row now lives in HeaderStack (continuous).
  • HeaderStackHow the university header and a unit's header share the top of a page, so a unit site never reads as two sites stacked.
  • SectionNavThe pages of one section beside the content, replacing nuin's red sidebars.
  • AlertBarThe one site-wide notice.
  • LocalFooterThe unit's own footer, directly above the GlobalFooter.
  • GlobalFooterThe footer every official site ends on, required and unaltered (brand).
  • BreadcrumbThe trail from the site home to this page, small and quiet above the title, ending on the current page as plain text with aria-current.
  • NavTriggerA top-level navigation item, 22a's .mnav-btn.
  • SiteIdentityThe unit’s name at the start of the local header, written out in full.
  • NavLinkA quiet navigation link for menus, section navigation and footers.
  • TableOfContents“On this page” for long policy and reference pages.
  • QuickLinksA page's short set of utility links (four to ten) as a ruled list.
  • ChapterRailThe page's chapters as one hairline rail of anchor links, each chapter a stretch of the line.
  • ContentsBarThe contents of a reading page as a row of pills that travels with the reader.
  • SiblingNavThe pages of one section as one quiet row of links under the hero ("About.

Heroes and page tops

The first thing on a page: its title, its picture or film, its one ask.

  • HeroPageThe photo hero of a section page.
  • ArticleHeaderThe top of a story.
  • ProfileHeaderThe top of a person’s page.
  • VenueHeaderThe hero of a page about a place people come to.
  • HeroStageThe headline on the page's own ground on the centre axis, a short lead and one primary pill, and under them one wide stage carrying a film or photograph that rises from the fold, so the first screen holds headline, lead, action and the top of the picture.
  • HeroFrameA full-window film or photograph under 22a's scene scrim, the headline at the largest display size low on the frame across eight columns, and the lead with its one primary pill in the right columns on the headline's last baseline (a film's glass pause control stands beside the pill).
  • HeroOffsetThe headline at the largest display size low on the left across eight columns.
  • TitleCardThe top of a Read or Operate page, where the type does the work and there is no picture.

Statements

One idea given a screen. Two per page at most.

  • StatementThe page’s opening pull-line as a component.
  • StatementBandOne large line as a whole band.
  • PullStatementA sentence pulled out of a paragraph, with the rest of the thought directly under it.
  • PictureBandA full-bleed picture or film with one large line set on it and at most one pill.
  • MilestoneTrackA giant word behind (the h2).
  • ShowcaseStageA pinned stage, one item a screen of scroll.
  • PhotoMarqueeA big centred heading over an endless rail of square paper prints at staggered heights, each with a small caption on its foot.

Media and text

A picture or film beside or around its words.

  • SectionHeaderThe heading that opens a section, with its lead and one onward ArrowLink.
  • MediaTextA photograph or film beside its copy on the 12-column grid, with a choice of split (media on 7, 8, 6 or 5 columns, either side), ratio (3:2, 4:5, 1:1, 16:9) and where the copy sits against the picture (top, centre, bottom), so a page's picture-and-copy sections vary on purpose.
  • ProseThe long-form body.
  • GalleryA set of photographs from one event or place.
  • StickyScenesA workhorse (compact chapters).
  • SpaceMosaicThe rooms of a place, shown rather than told.
  • FeatureRowsTwo to four big topics as a run of MediaText rows.
  • MediaStageOne picture or film given the full width of the page, its caption centred beneath.
  • OffsetPairTwo items, each a picture with its own short copy, in two forms.
  • MarginFiguresA tall picture with the section's heading and figures as one block beside it.
  • FlexPartsThe parts the flex family shares.

Sets and lists

Several things of one kind: grids, rails, rows, tabs and steps.

  • ExpandingCards22a’s research row.
  • CardGridA section of same-kind cards under one heading.
  • ProgramCardOne card for every kind of program a student can apply to.
  • AccordionA set of AccordionItems under one heading, with Expand all for readers who want everything.
  • TabsOne question answered several ways, one panel at a time.
  • TimelineDates in one process on a single rail.
  • StepsA numbered process, three to six Steps, with one call to action after the last.
  • DocumentListDownloads grouped by what they are for.
  • PathwayListThe few doors a visitor chooses between (Undergraduate, Graduate, Doctoral) set as display-size Light text links on full-width hairlines.
  • PathwayCardsA hub's front door to its two to four child pages.
  • PointListA short run of claims as hairline rows on the reading edge.
  • DirectoryGridA dense, logo-led directory of organisations.
  • PosterRailA set of parallel items as tall posters in one rail that starts on the content edge and runs off the right edge of the window.
  • PublicationListAn office’s reports and policy briefs as publications.
  • StageTabsTwo to six parallel items as one large stage and a row of pill tabs that swaps it.
  • IndexRowsFive to fourteen parallel items as an index of ruled rows in place of a wall of equal cards.
  • PillarIndexThe brand pillars as doors to their own pages.
  • NameWallA set of places or things by name (the campuses), each name a link that calls up its photograph and one line about it.
  • FilmstripA set of parallel items as tall 2:3 posters in one rail, each poster one link named by its title (an h3).
  • MixedRailA rail of cards of two widths, wide features (six columns) and narrow ones (three), on one picture height, so the row has a rhythm instead of a count.
  • RouteListThe ways people move through the global university system, as hairline rows.
  • UnevenGridExactly three, four or five parallel items when a grid is right.

Figures and proof

Numbers set as type with their sentence, and the marks of partners.

  • StatOne figure set as type, Light and tabular, with the sentence that makes it mean something, on a hairline rail.
  • StatBand22a’s research counters.
  • LogoWallA ruled sheet of partner LogoTiles.
  • ProofPointOutside evidence for a claim, set as proof rather than as a story.
  • StoryProofTwo to four claims, each proved by the Northeastern Global News story that shows it.
  • FactSheetThe university's headline figures in one place.
  • PartnerCasesTwo to four partnerships side by side, each as the research theme pages tell a case.
  • PartnerCasesPhotoPartnerCases with a photograph of each partnership's work at the head of its case, composed on the same list.
  • FigureLeadOne figure given a short section to itself.
  • FigureSlateA Night title card of figures.
  • LogoRowOne quiet row of partners' marks on the centre axis.

News and events

Stories and dates.

  • StorySpotlightOne Northeastern Global News story at the scale of a scene.
  • StoryLeadNortheastern Global News on a page at two clear scales.

Places and maps

Campuses, maps and globes.

  • GusGlobeThe global university system as one immersive Night chapter, lifted from 22a's globe scrolly.One page
  • CampusGlobeA set of places as a ruled list of names (each a link) beside a small globe.
  • CampusAtlasA flat map of North America (Albers equal-area) cropped to the set, a place across the Atlantic in a framed inset in the first clear corner.
  • GlobeViewThe one globe of the globes family.
  • GlobeHorizonA hero.
  • GlobeOrbitThe planet seen from the north, in the hand, under a centred heading, with the names in a centred row.
  • MapWallA statement (once a page).
  • GlobeTourA whole globe (never cut by its container) beside one campus at a time.
  • GlobeChaptersChapters of a set on columns 1-5 scroll past a globe pinned on 7-12.

Forms and finding

Fields, filters, finders, tables and dialogs.

  • FieldOne labelled form control.
  • ChoiceFieldCheckbox and radio rows.
  • SearchFieldSearch as one unit.
  • ChipGroupA labelled set of Chips that filters one facet.
  • InputSingle-line and multi-line text entry.
  • SelectA native select styled as a field, with the system chevron.
  • ChoiceThe checkbox and radio controls alone.
  • FilterPanelThe facets of a directory as one GET form.
  • DataTableA ruled table for facts people compare.
  • FinderThe whole directory as one organism.
  • DialogA native <dialog> for a task that needs the reader's whole attention for a moment.
  • ListToolbarThe line between a Finder's filters and its results.
  • FormAn inquiry form as one unit.

Closers and contact

How a page ends and how people reach us.

  • ContactBlockHow to reach an office.
  • ActionBandThe sitewide prefooter call to action.
  • CloserLineA workhorse closer, the quietest way to end a page.
  • CloserOffsetThe closing line anchored low and left with its sentence and primary pill as one block, and one tall picture off to the right that leaves the grid at the window's edge.
  • CloserSceneThe page ends the way the home page does, with a full-bleed photograph or film under a pool of shade, one line of display type on the centre axis, a sentence, and one primary pill.
  • CallListThe numbers a page exists to give, set as type.

The section, notices and the scripts patterns share.

  • RailControlsThe controls under every sideways rail.
  • NoticeA message about the page or the reader's task.
  • EmptyStateWhat a list says when it has nothing to show.
  • ToggletipAn info IconButton that reveals a short explanation in a small floating panel.
