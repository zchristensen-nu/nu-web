Events

  • page { direction }. Forward: the first item not yet wholly in view becomes the first one showing. Back: one screenful, landing on an item's edge. Nothing moves by itself.

Accessibility

  • Paddles are real buttons named for what they page. At an end a paddle is disabled; if it holds focus at that moment it says aria-disabled and stays focusable until focus leaves.
  • The ProgressLine is aria-hidden: the rail itself is a native scroller, reachable by keyboard and touch. The count is plain text.
  • An item that takes the keyboard's focus while cut off by either edge is brought wholly into view on the content edge.
  • Hidden without script, and whenever the rail cannot travel at least one item's width (a sliver of overflow needs no controls: the cut-off item says there is more).
  • With a mouse the track shows the grab cursor wherever it is not a link; a drag moves it 1:1 and never opens the card under the pointer.
  • Reduced motion: the ProgressLine does not travel with the scroll; it is set at once when the scroll comes to rest, and the paddles move the rail without a glide.
  • On paper the controls are gone and the rail prints as a grid of every item, three across.

Usage

  • Use it under every rail, on the content edge.
  • Name the noun: stories, people, campuses.
  • Use a rail for five to twelve items that are browsed: stories, films, people with pictures.
  • Restyle it per pattern, or move the paddles above the rail.
  • Auto-advance a rail.
  • Use a rail for a set people scan by name (campuses, colleges, offices): most names are out of sight; use IndexRows or NameWall.
  • Use a rail for three items: they fit the twelve columns (MixedRail fits three; on phones it stacks them), or use UnevenGrid.

It is removed when a sound film ends.
  • in view A loop plays only while a quarter of its frame is in the window and nothing above it is inert or hidden (a tab panel that is not showing); it never plays under reduced motion unless the reader presses play.
  • drift The root directly holds the video and its poster, so a holder puts data-drift on it (MediaStage, PictureBand do, for loops): the film travels, the controls do not.

Accessibility

  • The loop toggle is glass with a hairline ring (--film-control-ring): the dark disc reads on a bright frame, the ring on a dark one, 3:1 or better either way.

  • The play button covers the whole frame; on play, focus moves to the pause button in the bar.
  • A loop never plays under prefers-reduced-motion unless the reader presses play; it pauses when it leaves the window.
  • Without script a loop is its poster and a film with sound keeps its native controls and the designed ones stay hidden.

  • Give every sound film a captions track: without one it plays silent.
  • Choose a poster that is a frame of the film.
  • Autoplay sound.
  • Put text on a loop without a Scrim (PictureBand does it for you).
